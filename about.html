Skin care shouldn’t depend on where you live.

Most skin conditions can be treated once someone recognises them. The hard part is often the first step: knowing what you might be looking at, and whether it needs attention now.

EpivaScan is built for that step. It’s a tool for whoever is already there — a community health worker, a nurse, a family member — and never a replacement for a doctor.

How we’re building it.

  • Clear, not clinicalPlain percentages and plain language that anyone can read without training.
  • Honest about limitsWe say what the model can’t do as plainly as what it can, and will publish how it performs.
  • Photos are health dataA picture of someone’s skin is personal. Privacy is a design requirement from the start.
  • Built for the fieldMade for a phone in someone’s hand, in places where specialists are far away.

Questions.

Does EpivaScan diagnose skin disease?

No. It shows which conditions a photo most closely resembles, with a percentage for each. That is a starting point for a conversation with a health professional, who makes the diagnosis.

Can I use it today?

EpivaScan is still in development. The web demo shows the interface, but the AI model isn’t connected yet, so it can’t analyse photos.

Which conditions will it recognise?

The list is being finalised with the model and will be published with the model.

What happens to my photo?

In the current web demo your photo stays in your browser and is never uploaded. A full privacy policy will be published before the model goes live.

Does it work on a computer as well as a phone?

Yes. EpivaScan is designed for phones first and also runs in a web browser on a laptop or desktop.
